# The report

## Before you write

- Go through all the Mimic results for the case (`fetch-all-mimic-results`). See whether any email or phone hints match the results of other enrichments. Hints often show only the first and last characters or digits, so they won't match in length.
- If any enrichment seriously contradicts your findings, say so in the report and tell the user.
- Read the existing report first (`get-mcp-report`). Earlier rounds may have left reports on the same subject with information you don't have now. Consolidate them; don't duplicate.

## Writing it

- Write to the MCP report with `append-mcp-report`, or `edit-mcp-report` to replace it, under the heading "Final report for [subject]".
- Use headings and subheadings, following the template below.
- For every finding, say where it came from: the page, the enrichment or the clipping. The user has to be able to prove it.
- The report is for the user. Your working notes stay in project memory.

## Template: a person

Build a full profile of the person:

1. **Vitals**: date of birth or age, gender, name(s) and surname, aliases.
2. **Contact details**: email addresses, phone numbers, postal address.
3. **Social media**: links to every profile (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube channels and so on).
4. **Employment**: where they work now and where they've worked. Prominent people they worked with who shaped their thinking. Directorships.
5. **Travel and location**: where they are now. Have they moved around? Travelled internationally? Holidays, relocations for work.
6. **Assets and investments**: what they own (cars, boats, planes, houses and property, shares in companies, sports teams, collections, art) and investments they've made.
7. **Family**: by name and relationship. Spouses and children first, but also family who share employment or ownership with them.
8. **Personal relationships**: people likely to be close to them, romantically or professionally.
9. **Clubs and associations**: memberships, places they frequent. Meetups, civic groups, religious gatherings.
10. **Interests and motivations**: what they care about and spend their time on, what drives their work and public activity, and what they seem to stay away from.

The aim is an accurate picture of the person, with a source for every finding. You may draw conclusions the sources don't state outright; label them as your assessment.

## Template: a company, group, association or organisation

Build a full profile of the organisation:

1. **Name**: its full name, and any name it trades under.
2. **Type**: an NGO? A think tank? A commercial company? Really a government department? A global company or a corner shop?
3. **Contact details**: the postal address and location of each office, phone numbers. Offices around the world?
4. **Internet**: domain names, contact email, social media profiles.
5. **Funding and shareholding**: who paid for it? Who invested, people and companies? Venture capital? Bootstrapped by the founders? Government funded?
6. **Founders**: who started it. They may no longer work there; this is about who wanted it to exist.
7. **Management**: who runs it today, and how it's structured. Who reports to whom? Is there a board?
8. **Purpose**: what it's really for.
9. **Products and services**: what it actually sells or offers, and how it makes money.

If it's really a group of companies: tell the user, and if they want, repeat the profile for every company in the group.
